# Purpose

This document describes the ideas, beliefs, and values that shaped the Data Analytics Project Methodology (DAPM).

Unlike the architecture, which defines how the methodology works, the philosophy explains why the methodology exists.

---

# Why DAPM Exists

Many data analytics projects begin with tools.

The analyst imports a dataset, writes SQL queries, creates dashboards, or builds machine learning models without first understanding the business problem.

This often produces technically correct work that delivers little business value.

DAPM was created to solve this problem.

The methodology encourages analysts to begin with the business, understand stakeholder needs, prepare reliable data, and produce evidence-based recommendations that support business decisions.

The objective is not simply to analyse data.

The objective is to solve business problems.

---

# The DAPM Mindset

DAPM views analytics as a business discipline supported by technology.

Data, programming languages, databases, dashboards, and visualizations are tools that help answer business questions.

Technology is important, but it should never determine the direction of the analysis.

The business problem should always guide the analytical process.

---

# What DAPM Believes

## Business Creates the Question

Every analytics project begins because someone needs to make a decision.

The role of the analyst is to understand that decision before analysing data.

---

## Data Must Be Trusted

Reliable decisions require reliable data.

Understanding, profiling, cleaning, and validating data are essential before drawing conclusions.

Without trustworthy data, even the most sophisticated analysis can produce misleading results.

---

## Evidence Builds Confidence

Business recommendations should be supported by analytical evidence.

Assumptions, opinions, and intuition may help generate ideas, but decisions should ultimately be supported by validated findings.

---

## Documentation Is Part of the Solution

Documentation is not created after a project is completed.

It evolves throughout the project and becomes one of the project's deliverables.

Well-maintained documentation explains not only what was done, but also why important decisions were made.

---

## Methodologies Should Evolve

DAPM is not intended to remain static.

The methodology is improved through practical implementation, validation projects, and lessons learned from real-world analytics work.

Changes are introduced only after they have been validated through experience.

---

# Technology Independence

DAPM is independent of programming languages, databases, visualization tools, and analytics platforms.

The methodology can be applied using:

- Spreadsheets
- SQL
- Python
- R
- Power BI
- Tableau
- Other analytics platforms

The workflow remains the same even when the tools change.

---

# Who Should Use DAPM

DAPM is designed for anyone performing structured analytics work, including:

- Data Analysts
- Business Analysts
- Students
- Self-learners
- Consultants
- Analytics teams

The methodology supports client engagements, internal business projects, portfolio projects, academic case studies, and public datasets.

# Closing Statement

DAPM promotes a disciplined approach to analytics where business understanding guides technical implementation, reliable data supports analysis, and evidence drives recommendations.

The methodology continues to evolve through practical validation so that every improvement is supported by real-world experience rather than assumption.
